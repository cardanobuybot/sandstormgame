Every raid starts with {{n:scoutSeconds}} seconds in which nothing moves. The base is laid out in front of you, the clock for the battle has not started, and nothing you do costs anything. Most players spend those seconds waiting. This is a routine for spending them better.

{{shot:scout|Scouting. Everything you need is on this screen.}}

## What the screen tells you

**Top row:**

- A badge: **Player**, **Echo** or **Outpost**. A Player base is a real one and its owner loses what you take. An Echo is a copy of a real base whose owner loses nothing. An Outpost is built by the computer.
- The commander's name, Citadel level and trophies.
- The scouting clock, with an eye on it.

**Second row:**

- Destruction, at 0%, and three empty stars.
- **Oil and scrap on offer.** The number after the slash is everything this base can give you.
- **Tremors: low** or **Tremors: high**. High means the Sandmaw will come.

**In the corner:** a small radar of the whole map. Guns are red, oil and scrap buildings yellow, walls brown, the Citadel teal. Tap it to jump the camera there.

**Bottom:** your units with how many of each still fit, your army space, and two buttons: **Leave** and **Next**.

Tap any building and a panel shows its level, health and, for a gun, its damage, range and whether it hits ground, air or both.

## The 30-second routine

### Seconds 0 to 5: is this base worth it?

Three glances:

1. **The loot.** Compare it with what your own stores can take. If the game warns that your tanks or depots are full, the loot is not for you.
2. **The Citadel level.** A base one level above yours has stronger guns, one below has weaker ones. The loot figure on the screen already includes the bonus or penalty for the difference.
3. **The tremors.** Not a reason to skip, but it decides which army you bring.

If the loot is thin and you are not here for trophies, tap **Next**. It costs a little oil (5 at Citadel 1, up to 600 at Citadel 8) and no time.

### Seconds 5 to 15: where are the guns?

Look at the radar first: red dots are defenses. Then find them on the map and ask of each:

- **What can it hit?** Turrets and Shellpits: ground only. Flak Nests: air only. Rocket Batteries: both.
- **How far does it reach?** Tap it. A Shellpit reads "range 3–10": it cannot hit the first three cells around itself.
- **Is there anti-air at all?** If you have Flyers and the answer is no, you already know your army.

Remember what the radar does not show: Mines from Citadel 2, Sky Snares and Sand Blinds from Citadel 5. You will not know where they are until they go off.

### Seconds 15 to 25: where do I go in?

Now look at the walls and the loot together.

- **Trace the wall.** Is the ring closed? A gap is where your Infantry will walk, with or without your permission. If the defender left it on purpose, there is a Mine in it.
- **Find the stores.** Yellow on the radar. Oil Tanks, Scrap Depots and the Citadel hold the stored loot; Pumpjacks and Salvage Yards hold their own.
- **Pick the side** where the stores or the Citadel are closest to the edge and the fewest red dots are within reach.
- **Note the stragglers.** A Barracks or a Pumpjack far from everything else will need its own few soldiers at the end, if you want three stars.

### Seconds 25 to 30: choose the first unit

Select the unit you will land first, and decide where the second wave goes. Your first tap on the sand starts the battle, so there is no need to wait for the clock to run out. There is also no reason to hurry: an unused second of scouting is a free second of thinking.

## What "Tremors: high" changes

The worm arrives between {{n:wormFrom}} and {{n:wormTo}} seconds into the battle and hunts ground units. With high tremors:

- Prefer **aircraft** if the base's anti-air allows it.
- Land ground troops in **loose groups**, not one clump and not scattered singles.
- Keep a **reserve**, so the worm does not catch your whole army on the sand at once.

More in [how to beat the Sandmaw](/blog/how-to-beat-the-sandmaw/).

## Signs of a base that will bite

- **A tidy gap in an otherwise closed wall.** A funnel, with Mines.
- **An Oil Tank outside the walls with nothing near it.** From Citadel 5, the nothing may be a Sand Blind.
- **A Flak Nest in the exact centre** with Turrets around it. Leave the Flyers at home.
- **Guns bunched together, stores on the far side.** Good for loot, bad for stars.
- **Everything packed tight.** A present for Thunderwings, a problem for anyone else: every gun covers every building.

## Signs of an easy one

- **Stores on the edge.** Scavengers and Infantry empty them before the guns matter.
- **No Flak Nest**, at any level where you have aircraft.
- **Low walls on a high Citadel.** The owner rushed, and the guns are probably behind too. Tap one to check.
- **Buildings scattered wide.** No gun covers its neighbours.
- **The Echo badge.** You can attack without a thought for the owner, who loses nothing.

## Leave, Next, or attack?

- **Leave** costs nothing before your first unit lands. The raid never happened.
- **Next** costs a little oil and shows you another base straight away.
- **Attack** when you can say in one sentence what you are going to do: "Rams at the north wall, Infantry behind, Flyers on the Shellpit, three soldiers saved for the Barracks in the corner."

If you cannot say that sentence, look for another five seconds. The plan itself is the subject of the [attack guide](/blog/how-to-get-three-stars-attack-guide/).
