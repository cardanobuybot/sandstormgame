Dune Clash runs on two resources, and each has its own job. If you understand which is which, you will never again sit with full oil tanks and not a single wall.

## Two resources, two jobs

| | Oil | Scrap |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Comes from | Pumpjacks, and raids | Salvage Yards, and raids |
| Stored in | The Citadel and Oil Tanks | The Citadel and Scrap Depots |
| Pays for | The Citadel, Pumpjacks, Salvage Yards, tanks and depots, Barracks, Motor Works, Rotor Pad, Proving Ground, all unit research, skipping an opponent | Turrets, Shellpits, Flak Nests, Sand Blinds, Rocket Batteries, walls, Mines, Sky Snares |

In short: **oil grows the base and the army, scrap defends it.** You cannot spend one in place of the other, and there is nowhere to trade them.

The story behind it: the planet has oil under the sand, and the wrecks of ships that fell on it lie on top. A Salvage Yard is a crew with cutting torches taking one of those ships apart.

## What your base makes on its own

Pumpjacks and Salvage Yards work around the clock, whether the game is open or not.

{{table:economy|wide}}

Three details decide how much of that you really get:

1. **A producer stops when it is full.** At every level a Pumpjack or Salvage Yard holds exactly {{b:collector.1.fullAfter}} of its own output. Come back after twelve hours and you have lost half.
2. **A producer makes nothing while it is being upgraded.** Upgrade them one or two at a time.
3. **Collecting needs room.** If your tanks are full, oil stays in the Pumpjack, where a raider can take half of it.

One tap on **Collect all** empties every Pumpjack and Salvage Yard at once.

## Storage: the wall you hit without noticing

You can never hold more than your stores allow, and three things depend on that:

- **Big purchases must fit.** Citadel 6 costs {{b:citadel.6.cost}} oil. At Citadel 5, with both Oil Tanks at their top level, you can hold {{store:oil.5}}. With one tank a level behind, you cannot buy it at all.
- **Loot must fit.** Whatever a raid wins beyond your free room is left behind. The result screen tells you how much.
- **Collecting must fit**, as above.

So storage comes first in every upgrade plan, even though it is the least exciting thing to build.

## Raiding pays better than waiting

Compare your own income with what one good raid brings. The table shows a full raid on an **Echo** of your own Citadel level, which is the modest case: a real, neglected base usually holds more.

{{table:echo-loot}}

A raid takes three and a half minutes, scouting included. At every level, **one clean raid is worth between 25 minutes and an hour of pumping.** Your pumps are the steady background; raids are how you actually pay for a Citadel.

Your troops are free, so the only cost of raiding is your time, plus a little oil if you skip opponents with **Next**.

## How not to lose it

Everything in your stores can be raided, within limits:

- From stored oil and scrap, a raid can take at most {{n:lootShareLow}}% (Citadel 1 to 4), 16% (Citadel 5 and 6) or {{n:lootShareTop}}% (Citadel 7 and 8).
- From Pumpjacks and Salvage Yards, a raid can take {{n:lootProducers}}% of what sits inside.
- If a raid takes {{n:shieldShare}}% or more of what there was to take, you get a **shield** for {{n:shieldHours}} hours, and raids during a shield cost you nothing.

Which gives three habits:

1. **Spend before you leave.** Resources turned into buildings cannot be stolen. Start your biggest upgrade just before you close the game.
2. **Empty producers when you arrive and when you leave.** Half of a full Pumpjack is a present for the next raider.
3. **Spend scrap on walls.** Walls are built at once and need no builder, so there is always a way to turn spare scrap into something permanent.

The exact loot rules, with examples, are in the [loot guide](/blog/loot-guide-how-much-oil-and-scrap-can-you-steal/).

## What to upgrade first

At a new Citadel level, in this order:

1. The **new Pumpjack or Salvage Yard** the level allows. A new one adds more than a level on an old one.
2. **Oil Tanks and Scrap Depots**, until the next Citadel fits.
3. **Pumpjacks and Salvage Yards** to their top level, one at a time.

If one resource keeps running out, check the balance. Players usually have too much oil and too little scrap, because Pumpjacks come first in the build menu and there is always one more of them. From Citadel 5 on, the guns and walls of a level cost more scrap than all its other buildings cost oil.

## Three ways to get resources in a hurry

- **Raid a base above your level.** A base one Citadel level higher pays ×1.2, two levels higher ×1.5. The bonus is added by the game; the defender does not lose more.
- **Take revenge.** A revenge raid always holds at least as much oil as was taken from you. See [trophies, shields and revenge](/blog/trophies-ladder-shields-explained/).
- **Crystals** can buy the oil or scrap you are short of for one purchase. It is rarely the best use of them: the [crystals guide](/blog/dune-clash-crystals-guide/) explains why.
