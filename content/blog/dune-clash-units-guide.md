Dune Clash has nine units: two kinds of foot soldiers, five vehicles and two aircraft. This guide lists what each one does, its numbers at every level, and what kills it.

Three rules apply to all of them:

- **Units are free.** There is nothing to train and nothing to wait for. In every raid you may land any mix of unlocked units that fits your army space.
- **Army space is the budget.** Barracks, the Motor Works and the Rotor Pad each add space. A rifleman takes 1, a heavy bomber takes 12.
- **Units think for themselves.** You choose who lands and where. After that each unit picks its own targets, and each kind has its own taste.

"Cells" are squares of the map: a wall is one cell, a turret is 2×2, the Citadel is 4×4.

## How units get stronger

Units have six levels. You raise them one at a time in the **Proving Ground**, which you can build from Citadel 2. Research costs oil and takes time, and only one unit can be researched at once. A Proving Ground of level 1 lets units reach level 2, and so on up to a level 5 Proving Ground and level 6 units.

An upgrade adds roughly an eighth to health and damage each time. That sounds small, but level 6 is about 75% tougher and harder-hitting than level 1 for the same army space.

{{unit:infantry}}

The first unit you get and one you never stop using. One Infantry is weak; forty of them are a tide. They are the cheapest way to soak up shots, clear buildings outside the walls and finish a base that has already lost its guns.

**Watch out for:** anything that hits an area. A Shellpit shell or a Mine can remove a whole cluster at once, and Turrets do one and a half times their damage to light units. Spread your Infantry along an edge instead of dropping them on one spot. The Sandmaw loves a tight crowd too.

{{unit-levels:infantry}}

{{unit:shooter}}

Rangers carry rocket tubes. They fire from 3.5 cells away, so they can shoot over a wall at the gun behind it, and they do half as much again to defenses. They have less health than Infantry and take twice the space.

**Use them** behind something tougher. A line of Infantry or a Bulwark in front, Rangers a few steps back, and guns fall quickly. **Watch out for** Shellpits, which outrange them by a long way.

{{unit-levels:shooter}}

{{unit:breaker}}

The Ram is a tracked wedge that does twelve times its damage to walls. Its path-finding barely counts walls as an obstacle, so it drives more or less straight at its target and opens a gap for everyone behind it.

**Use it** first, where you want the hole, with the troops that will use the hole landing right after. **Watch out for** Sand Blinds and Rocket Batteries: both do extra damage to armoured vehicles.

{{unit-levels:breaker}}

{{unit:scavenger}}

A fast buggy, the fastest thing on the ground. It heads for Pumpjacks, Oil Tanks, Salvage Yards and Scrap Depots before anything else and does double damage to them. If you are raiding for loot and do not care about stars, this is your unit.

**Use it** in packs, on the side of the base where the stores are, once the nearest Turret is busy with something else. **Watch out:** it counts as a light unit, so Turrets shred it.

{{unit-levels:scavenger}}

{{unit:flyer}}

A gyrocopter and your first aircraft. Walls mean nothing to it, Turrets and Shellpits cannot shoot it, Mines do not notice it, and the Sandmaw cannot reach it. It goes for defenses first.

**Use it** against bases with one Flak Nest or none, or after your ground troops have knocked the Flak out. **Watch out for** Flak Nests, Sky Snares (hidden until an aircraft passes over), Sand Blinds and Rocket Batteries.

{{unit-levels:flyer}}

{{unit:bulwark}}

A heavy tank with more than twenty times the health of an Infantry soldier. It goes for defenses, chews through walls at triple damage, and gives everything around it time to work.

**Use it** in front. Two or three Bulwarks landing first will pull the fire of every gun in reach. **Watch out for** its size: ten army space each. And it is slow, so the Sandmaw catches it easily.

{{unit-levels:bulwark}}

{{unit:longshot}}

Rocket artillery with a reach of nine cells. That is more than a Turret or a Sand Blind can answer, so a Longshot can stand outside their range and take them apart. It fires slowly and has little health.

**Use it** behind a screen, against bases that lean on Turrets. **Watch out for** Shellpits and Rocket Batteries: both reach ten cells, one more than the Longshot.

{{unit-levels:longshot}}

{{unit:wrench}}

The only unit that cannot fight. A Wrench Rig follows your ground vehicles and repairs the most damaged one within three cells. It does not repair foot soldiers or aircraft.

**Use it** with Bulwarks and Rams: one or two Wrench Rigs behind a tank column can keep it alive through a whole base. A battle ends once only Wrench Rigs are left, so they never win one alone.

{{unit-levels:wrench}}

{{unit:thunderwing}}

A heavy bomber, the last unit you unlock. Slow, tough, and every bomb hits everything within 1.6 cells of where it lands. Like the Flyer it ignores walls, Turrets, Shellpits, Mines and the worm.

**Use it** where buildings stand close together. **Watch out for** its price: twelve army space. Two Flak Nests covering each other will bring a few Thunderwings down before they do their work.

{{unit-levels:thunderwing}}

## Which unit beats which defense

| If the base has a lot of | Bring | Leave at home |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Turrets | Longshots, Bulwarks, aircraft | Scavengers and bare Infantry |
| Shellpits | Fast units and aircraft; get inside 3 cells | Slow clumps of foot soldiers |
| Flak Nests | Ground troops | Flyers and Thunderwings |
| Sand Blinds and Rocket Batteries | Infantry and Rangers in numbers | Lone tanks |
| Walls | Rams, Bulwarks, aircraft | Nothing: just bring a way through |
| Tremors (the worm is coming) | Aircraft, or enough guns to kill it | A slow, all-ground army in one clump |
