Loot is why you raid and what you lose when you are raided. The rules are the same in both directions, and they are simple enough to do in your head. This page lays them out with worked examples.

## The short version

- A raid can take a **share of what is stored** (20%, 16% or 14%, depending on the defender's Citadel) and **half of what sits in Pumpjacks and Salvage Yards**.
- That amount is multiplied by a factor for the **difference in Citadel levels**.
- You earn loot **as you damage the buildings that hold it**.
- What does **not fit in your own stores** is left behind.

## Step 1: how much is on offer

{{table:loot-share}}

The stored part is spread over the buildings that can hold it, in proportion to their size: oil over the Oil Tanks and the Citadel, scrap over the Scrap Depots and the Citadel. The other part sits in each Pumpjack and Salvage Yard.

**Example.** A Citadel 4 base has 60,000 oil in store and 8,000 oil waiting in its Pumpjacks.

- From storage: 20% of 60,000 = 12,000.
- From the Pumpjacks: half of 8,000 = 4,000.
- On offer: **16,000 oil**. The other 52,000 cannot be touched by this raid, however well it goes.

Scrap is worked out the same way, separately.

## Step 2: the Citadel difference

{{table:loot-level}}

Raiding down is poor business: a base two Citadel levels below yours gives half. Raiding up pays a bonus. Ordinary matchmaking stays within one level of your own; bigger gaps turn up in revenge raids and now and then with Echoes.

Two things worth knowing:

- **The bonus comes from the game, not from the defender.** If you raid a base one level above you, you get 120% and they lose 100%.
- **The penalty protects the defender.** A raider one level above you gets 80%, and you lose only that 80%.

**Example, continued.** The same base raided by a Citadel 5 player offers 16,000 × 0.8 = 12,800 oil. Raided by a Citadel 3 player, 16,000 × 1.2 = 19,200.

You never have to do this sum during a raid. The loot figure on the scouting screen already includes it.

## Step 3: earning it

Each building holds its own part of the loot, and gives it up **in proportion to the damage it takes**. Shoot an Oil Tank down to half health and you have half of what it held. Destroy it and you have all of it.

That has practical consequences:

- **You do not need stars to get rich.** A raid that reaches the tanks, drains them and tails off at 40% can bring home most of the oil.
- **Scavengers exist for this.** They head for oil and scrap buildings before anything else and deal double damage to them.
- **Where the loot is decides where to land.** Tanks, depots and the Citadel hold the stored share. A base with full Pumpjacks on the edge is a quick snack without going inside at all.

The two counters at the top of the battle screen rise as you damage the buildings that hold loot, and show what you have earned so far out of the total.

## Step 4: bringing it home

Loot has to fit. If your Oil Tanks have room for 5,000 and you win 12,000, you get 5,000.

The game tells you twice. While scouting: "Your tanks have room for only 5,000 of this oil." On the result screen: "+5,000 of 12,000", with a note about what was left behind.

The defender loses what you earned in the battle either way. Loot that does not fit is simply gone, for both of you. **Spend before you raid.**

## Echoes, Outposts and shielded bases

Not every base you raid is a live one.

- An **Echo** is a copy of a real base. Its owner loses nothing.
- An **Outpost** is built by the computer.
- A real base **under a shield** can still come up as an opponent, and its owner loses nothing either.

In all three cases the game supplies the loot itself, as if the stores were about a third full. If an Echo or a shielded base really holds more than that, you get the real amount, still without costing the owner anything.

{{table:echo-loot}}

A full, neglected real base usually pays better than these. That is deliberate: raiding real players is where the big hauls are.

## How to lose less

Turn the rules around and they become a defense plan.

**1. Keep little in the pumps.** Half of an uncollected Pumpjack is on offer, against a fifth or less of a tank. Oil is safer in storage than in the pump. Tap **Collect all** when you arrive and before you leave.

**2. Spend down before you log off.** The share is taken from what you have. 14% of nothing is nothing.

**3. Protect the tanks, not the pumps.** A raider has to damage the building to get its loot. Stores in the middle, behind walls and under guns, give up less than stores on the edge. See the [layout guide](/blog/dune-clash-base-layout-guide/).

**4. Let the shield work.** A raid that takes {{n:shieldShare}}% or more of what was on offer gives you a shield for {{n:shieldHours}} hours. One bad raid is followed by a quiet evening.

**5. Do not fear high-level raiders.** They get a fraction, and so you lose a fraction.

## How much can you lose at worst?

Take the worst case: a full raid by a player of your own Citadel level, every store destroyed, no shield.

- At **Citadel 1 to 4**: a fifth of what you have stored, plus half of what is in the pumps.
- At **Citadel 5 and 6**: 16%, plus half of the pumps.
- At **Citadel 7 and 8**: 14%, plus half of the pumps.

Then a shield, because a raid like that passes the {{n:shieldShare}}% mark easily. A single raid cannot take most of your oil. A week of never collecting and never spending can.

## Loot and revenge

When someone raids you, the **Log** offers **Revenge** for {{n:revengeHours}} hours. A revenge raid holds at least as much oil as was taken from you, whatever the raider's stores look like now. It hits a copy of their base, so they lose nothing: you get your oil back from the game. More in [trophies, shields and revenge](/blog/trophies-ladder-shields-explained/).
