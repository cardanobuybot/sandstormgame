If you typed "games like Clash of Clans" into a search box, you already know the loop you enjoy: build a base, defend it, raid other people for loot, climb. Clash of Clans, released by Supercell in 2012, made that loop famous, and dozens of games have followed it since.

Dune Clash is one of them, and a very new one. We make it, so this is not a neutral review. What we can do is be exact about what is the same, what is different and what is missing, so that you can decide in two minutes whether it is worth a download.

## What Dune Clash keeps

- **A base that grows over weeks.** A central building, the Citadel, has {{n:citadelMax}} levels. Each one unlocks new buildings and units and lets the rest go higher.
- **Timers and builders.** Upgrades take real time, and you can run only as many as you have builders.
- **Raids on other players' bases.** You attack a base while its owner is away; they watch the replay later.
- **Three stars.** One for half the base, one for the main building, one for everything.
- **Trophies and a ladder.** Win stars, gain trophies, move up.
- **Shields and revenge.** A base that loses a lot is protected for {{n:shieldHours}} hours, and you can hit back at whoever raided you.

If you have played any game of this kind, you will know what to do in the first minute.

## What it changes

### Your army is free and always ready

There is no training screen. Units cost nothing and take no time. Each raid you pick any mix of the units you have unlocked, up to your army space, and land them. Lose them all and the next raid starts with a full army again. The limit on how much you play is your own time, not a queue.

### A third side: the Sandmaw

In about half of all raids, a giant sand worm arrives between {{n:wormFrom}} and {{n:wormTo}} seconds into the fight and goes for anything walking or driving on the ground. It ignores buildings and cannot reach aircraft. A reading while you scout tells you whether it is coming. It changes how you choose an army and where you land it, and it can be killed. [More about the worm](/blog/how-to-beat-the-sandmaw/).

### Infantry, vehicles and aircraft, and guns that are blind to some of them

Turrets and Shellpits cannot shoot aircraft. Flak Nests shoot nothing else. Some guns do extra damage to light units and some to armour. A Shellpit cannot hit anything closer than three cells. So "bring more" is rarely the answer; "bring the right thing" is. See the [units guide](/blog/dune-clash-units-guide/) and the [defenses guide](/blog/dune-clash-defenses-guide/).

### Two resources with separate jobs

Oil pays for the Citadel, the economy, army buildings and research. Scrap pays for everything that defends. You cannot starve your defenses to feed your economy, or the other way round.

### Results are checked on the server

Your phone sends only the list of what you landed and where. The server plays the whole battle again and works out the stars and the loot itself. [How that stops cheating](/blog/fair-play-server-replays-anti-cheat/).

### No ads, and a browser version

There is not one advert in Dune Clash. The game earns money only from optional packs of crystals. And you can try it in a browser tab before you install anything.

## What it does not have

It would be easy to leave this part out. Here it is anyway.

- **No clans, no clan wars, no chat.** For many players that is the heart of the genre. Clans are the next large feature we plan to build, but they are not in the game today.
- **No heroes and no spells.** Battles are decided by which units you bring and where you land them.
- **Far fewer players.** The game came out in November 2026. While the player pool is small, some of the bases you raid are copies of real bases whose owners lose nothing (labelled "Echo") or computer-built bases (labelled "Outpost").
- **Less content.** {{n:buildingCount}} buildings, {{n:unitCount}} units, {{n:citadelMax}} Citadel levels. A game that has been growing for more than a decade has much more of everything.
- **English only**, for now.
- **Android is still in testing.** iPhone and the browser version are open to everyone.

## Who will like Dune Clash

- You want the build-and-raid loop **without a huge time commitment**. All the building work in the game adds up to about {{n:buildDays}} days for one builder, and you have up to four. That is a few months of casual play, not a few years.
- You like **picking an army to fit a base** more than collecting and levelling heroes.
- You want something you can **try in a browser tab** before installing anything.
- You enjoy being early in a small game and watching it grow.

## Who should wait

- You play these games **for the clan**. Come back when clans arrive.
- You want **hundreds of hours of content** on day one.
