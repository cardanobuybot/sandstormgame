A wall does no damage and kills nothing. Its whole job is to make enemy troops spend time, in a place of your choosing, while your guns shoot them. This guide covers both sides of it: building walls that work, and getting through someone else's.

{{gallery:wall}}

## The numbers

{{levels:wall}}

How many walls you may place depends on the Citadel: 20 at level 1, then 50, 75, 100, 125, 150, 175 and {{n:walls}} at level 8.

Four things make walls different from every other building:

- **They are built at once and need no builder.** That goes for upgrades too.
- **They cost scrap**, like all defenses.
- **They do not count towards destruction.** An attacker gets nothing for breaking them: no percentage, no stars, no loot.
- **Shots fly over them.** A wall stops feet and wheels, never bullets. A Ranger behind a wall can still hit a building three cells away.

## How units decide: through or around?

Every ground unit looks for the quickest way to its target. A wall in the way is not a dead end; it counts as a detour of a certain length. If walking around is shorter than that, the unit walks around. If not, it breaks the wall that stands in its path.

How long a detour each unit will accept before it starts breaking:

| Unit | A wall counts as a detour of | In practice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ram | 1 cell | Drives straight through almost any wall |
| Bulwark | 4 cells | Pushes through unless a gap is right there |
| Infantry | 12 cells | Looks for a gap first, breaks in only when the way round is long |
| Scavenger | 14 cells | Goes around |
| Ranger | 16 cells | Goes around, or shoots over |
| Longshot | 20 cells | Almost never breaks a wall; it fires over them from far away |
| Wrench Rig | cannot break walls | Waits for a gap someone else makes |
| Flyer, Thunderwing | – | Fly over |

Two more rules:

- **Nobody squeezes through a diagonal.** Two walls that touch only at their corners are as tight as two walls side by side.
- **When a wall falls, everyone notices.** Ground units look for a new route the moment anything is destroyed, so troops that were walking round will turn and pour through a fresh breach.

## How many hits a wall takes

{{table:wall-break}}

The Ram deals twelve times its damage to walls and the Bulwark three times. Read the last row: a level 8 wall takes a level 1 Ram eight hits, under ten seconds. A single level 1 Infantry soldier needs 183 hits, which is the whole battle.

That is the point of walls. Against an army with no Rams, every piece is worth many seconds of free shooting for your guns.

## Building walls that work

**1. Close the ring.** A wall with a gap is a signpost. Infantry, Scavengers and Rangers will happily walk twelve cells to use it. Check every corner: a missing piece makes the whole ring pointless.

**2. Wall what matters, not everything.** At Citadel 3 you have 75 pieces. That is one good ring around the Citadel, your stores and your best guns, not a fence around the whole base. Leave Barracks and the Proving Ground outside: they hold no loot.

**3. Keep guns inside, within reach of the wall.** A wall only helps if a gun is shooting at whoever is stuck on it. A Turret reaches 6.5 to 7.5 cells; a wall further away than that from every gun is decoration.

**4. Build compartments when you can afford them.** From Citadel 5 or so you have enough pieces to split the inside into two or three rooms. A Ram that breaks the outer ring then opens one room, not the whole base.

**5. Do not hug the buildings.** Leave a cell or two between the wall and what it protects. Rangers reach 3.5 cells: with the wall tight against an Oil Tank they shoot the tank over the wall without ever needing to get in.

**6. Use a gap on purpose, or not at all.** One deliberate opening with Mines in it and two Turrets watching is a trap. An accidental one is a gift. See [hidden defenses](/blog/traps-guide-mines-sand-blinds-sky-snares/).

## Upgrading walls

Walls are the big scrap sink of the late game. One piece from level 1 to level 8 costs 16,090 scrap; all {{n:walls}} cost more than three million.

- Upgrade the **inner ring first**: the pieces around the Citadel and the stores.
- Upgrade in **whole sections**. Rams hit whatever is in their path, but Infantry will chew through wherever they arrive, and one weak piece is where they get in.
- Use walls to **soak up spare scrap** before you log off. They are instant, and scrap in a wall cannot be raided.

Remember that removing a wall gives back only {{n:refund}}% of what it cost, while moving one is free. Rearrange as often as you like; do not demolish.

## Breaking walls as the attacker

- **Bring Rams from Citadel 3 on.** Two or three are enough for most bases. Land them first, at the spot where you want the breach, and land the rest of the army behind them a few seconds later.
- **Give the Rams company.** They have decent health but no real damage against anything but walls, and guns shoot the nearest target. A Bulwark or a handful of Infantry in front keeps them alive.
- **Or skip the walls.** Flyers and Thunderwings ignore them. Longshots outrange Turrets from outside. Against a base with strong walls and weak anti-air, do not knock: fly.
- **Look for the gap.** In the thirty seconds of scouting, trace the wall with your eye. If there is an opening, your Infantry will find it, and so will the defender's Mines.
