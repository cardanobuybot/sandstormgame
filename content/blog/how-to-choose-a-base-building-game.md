A puzzle game costs you an evening. A base-building game can ask for a year. You will not know in the first hour whether it deserves that: the first hour of every game in this genre is generous, fast and fun. The differences show up in week three.

Here are seven questions that reveal them early. We make one of these games, so after each question we answer it for Dune Clash. Use the same list on any other game and see what you find.

## 1. What exactly does money buy?

Almost every game of this kind is free and sells something. The question is what.

- **Time** is the mildest answer: you pay to skip waiting that a patient player sits out.
- **Power** is the harsh one: units, heroes or gear that are stronger than anything a free player can have.
- **Slots** sit in between: extra builders, extra queues.

Look in the shop before you invest a week. Read the names of the things for sale.

**Dune Clash:** only time. Crystals finish timers and top up oil or scrap. Units, buildings and all four builders come from playing. [The prices are public](/blog/dune-clash-crystals-guide/).

## 2. How long does it take, really?

Ask what the last upgrade costs. Early timers are measured in seconds; if the last ones are measured in weeks, the game is asking for years.

Neither answer is wrong. Some people want a hobby for five years. Just know which you are signing up for.

**Dune Clash:** the longest single job is three days. All the building work in the game adds up to about {{n:buildDays}} days for one builder and there are up to four, so a complete base is a few months of casual play. That is short for the genre. Whether it is a strength or a weakness depends on what you want. [The full sums are here](/blog/free-to-play-guide/).

## 3. What happens to my base when I am away?

Every one of these games lets other players attack you while you are offline. Find out:

- **How much can one attack take?** Everything, or a share?
- **Do I have to rebuild or repair afterwards?**
- **Is there protection after a bad hit?**

A game where one unlucky night wipes out a week is a game you will come to resent.

**Dune Clash:** a raid can take at most 14 to 20% of what you have stored, depending on your Citadel, and half of what sits uncollected in your pumps. Nothing is destroyed for good and there are no repairs. A heavy loss brings an {{n:shieldHours}}-hour shield. [The rules, with examples](/blog/loot-guide-how-much-oil-and-scrap-can-you-steal/).

## 4. Can results be faked?

Popular games attract cheats. The important question is where the result of a battle is decided: on the player's phone, which the player controls, or on the company's server.

You can rarely see this from outside, but a game that publishes how it works is usually one that has thought about it.

**Dune Clash:** your phone sends only the list of what you landed and when. The server plays the whole battle again and awards the stars and loot itself. [We wrote that up in detail](/blog/fair-play-server-replays-anti-cheat/).

## 5. Is it a game for one, or for a group?

Many of these games are really about the clan: chat, shared wars, donated troops. For some players that is the whole point. For others it is pressure: the game stops being something you play and becomes something you owe.

Check whether the social side is optional, and whether there is anything to do without it.

**Dune Clash:** there are **no clans and no chat today**. It is a solo game with a ladder. Clans are the next big feature we intend to build. If the clan is why you play this genre, this is the wrong moment for Dune Clash, and we would rather say so.

## 6. How does it treat my attention?

Three things to look at:

- **Adverts.** Are there any? Are rewards tied to watching them?
- **Notifications.** How many a day, and can you switch them off?
- **Daily chores.** Is there a list of tasks you must do every day or fall behind?

A game can be free and respectful, or free and exhausting.

**Dune Clash:** no adverts at all, no notifications and no daily task list. One small daily reward exists: {{n:dailyCrystals}} crystals for your first won raid of the day.

## 7. Who made it, and can I leave?

Finally, the practical questions:

- **Is there a privacy policy you can read in five minutes?** What does the game store about you?
- **Can you delete your account from inside the game?**
- **Can you move your progress to a new phone?**
- **Is there a name and a contact address behind it?**

**Dune Clash:** it is made by Sandstorm Game, a one-person independent studio. The game stores no name, e-mail address or phone number: you play as a guest with a commander name you choose. **Delete account** is in the menu, and so is saving your base to Game Center or Google. [The privacy policy](https://dune-of-clash.vercel.app/privacy.html) is one page.

## The scorecard

Copy this and fill it in for any game you are considering.

| Question | What to look for | Dune Clash |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What does money buy? | Time, not power | Time only |
| How long is it? | An honest answer | A few months |
| What can I lose offline? | A capped share, no rebuilding | 14 to 20% of stores, nothing destroyed |
| Can results be faked? | Decided on the server | Every raid replayed on the server |
