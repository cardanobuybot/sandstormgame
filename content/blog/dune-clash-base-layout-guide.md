Your base is attacked when you are not looking. It cannot react, improvise or ask for help: it does whatever its layout makes it do. The good news is that moving buildings is free and instant, so a better layout costs nothing but ten minutes of thought.

{{screen:1|A base at Citadel 5: stores and Citadel inside the wall, guns around them.}}

## First decide what you are protecting

A raid can take three things from you, and you cannot defend all of them equally.

| You want to keep | What the raider needs | So protect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Oil and scrap** | To damage the buildings that hold them | Oil Tanks, Scrap Depots, the Citadel, then Pumpjacks and Salvage Yards |
| **Trophies** | Stars: half your buildings, your Citadel, or everything | The Citadel, and enough buildings that 50% is hard |
| **Nothing in particular** | – | Then build for loot. Trophies come back; a lost Citadel upgrade does not. |

Most players should build for loot. Stored oil and scrap sit in the tanks, the depots and the Citadel, shared out by how much each can hold; half of what a Pumpjack or Salvage Yard has pumped sits in the pump itself. And loot leaves a building **as it takes damage**, not only when it falls. A tank that is shot to half health has given up half of its share.

## The four rings

Think of the base as rings, from the middle out.

**1. The core: Citadel and stores.** They hold the loot and one of the three stars. They go in the middle, as far from every edge as the map allows.

**2. The guns.** Around the core and close to it, so that their circles overlap *over the stores*. A Turret reaches about 7 cells. A gun whose circle does not cover something worth stealing is guarding sand.

**3. The wall.** Around the guns and the core, with a cell or two of space inside, so that Rangers cannot shoot the stores over it.

**4. Everything else.** Barracks, the Proving Ground, the Motor Works and the Rotor Pad hold no loot. Put them outside the wall, spread around all sides.

## Why the outside buildings matter

Those outside buildings do two jobs.

**They soak up the first wave.** Infantry, Rangers and Rams attack the nearest building. A Barracks standing between the edge and your wall is where they stop first, inside the reach of your guns and outside your wall.

**They decide the 50% star.** Destruction is counted per building. If more than half of your buildings stand outside the wall in easy reach, any raider gets a star for free. If you are playing for trophies, keep the count of outside buildings below half, or make them awkward: far apart, on all four sides, each inside the circle of a gun.

## Where each gun goes

- **Turrets:** on the ring, facing the sides raiders are likely to choose, which means the sides nearest your stores. Two that overlap beat two that do not.
- **Shellpit:** in the middle. It reaches 10 cells and cannot hit anything within 3, so the centre is the only place where its blind ring is covered by everything else.
- **Flak Nest:** in the middle too, behind walls. It is the first thing a raider with aircraft wants dead.
- **Sand Blind:** near the place where vehicles will break in, or beside the store closest to the edge.
- **Rocket Battery:** central, where its 10 cells reach every side.

The reasoning for each is in the [defenses guide](/blog/dune-clash-defenses-guide/).

## Walls

Three rules cover most of it:

1. **A closed ring or nothing.** One missing piece and the Infantry walk in.
2. **Diagonals are sealed.** Two pieces touching at the corners let nobody through, so a diagonal run saves pieces.
3. **The wall must be inside a gun's circle**, or nobody is shooting at whoever is breaking it.

At Citadel 3 you have {{limit:wall.3}} pieces; at Citadel 8, {{limit:wall.8}}. The [walls guide](/blog/walls-guide/) shows how to spend them.

## Mines and snares

Hidden defenses belong where troops *will* go, not where they might.

- Mines in any gap, and on the short path between the edge and your stores.
- Sky Snares on the straight line from the edge to your nearest guns, because aircraft fly at defenses first.
- Never side by side: one soldier sets off both.

See [hidden defenses](/blog/traps-guide-mines-sand-blinds-sky-snares/).

## Layout by Citadel level

**Citadel 1 and 2.** You have one or two Turrets and 20 to 50 wall pieces. Put the Citadel, the Oil Tank and the Scrap Depot together, the Turrets right beside them, and use the walls as a tight box around the stores. Everything else outside.

**Citadel 3 and 4.** 75 to 100 pieces: enough for one honest ring around the Citadel, all stores and the Shellpit. The Flak Nest goes inside it at Citadel 4.

**Citadel 5 and 6.** 125 to 150 pieces. Split the inside into two rooms, so one breach does not open everything. Add the Sand Blind near the likeliest breach and the Rocket Battery at the centre.

**Citadel 7 and 8.** 175 to 200 pieces and Thunderwings overhead. Leave one empty cell between stores, so that a bomb on one does not reach the next, and keep the two Flak Nests apart so that one push cannot kill both.

## Let the replays design your base

Every raid on you is saved. Open **Log**, find the raid under **Defenses** and tap **Replay**. You see the whole fight, including your own Mines and hidden guns.

Watch three things:

1. **Where did they land?** That side is where your base looks weakest. Move a Turret, or move the stores away from it.
2. **What did they reach in the first thirty seconds?** If it was an Oil Tank, it is too close to the edge.
3. **Which gun never fired?** It is in the wrong place. Which Mine never went off? Same.

Change one thing, and watch the next replay. Three or four rounds of this beat any layout copied from someone else, because it is fitted to the raiders you actually meet.

## Before you log off

A layout protects a share of your loot, never all of it. The surest protection is to **have little to take**: collect from your Pumpjacks and Salvage Yards, start the biggest upgrade you can afford, and put spare scrap into walls. What has been spent cannot be stolen. The numbers are in the [loot guide](/blog/loot-guide-how-much-oil-and-scrap-can-you-steal/).
