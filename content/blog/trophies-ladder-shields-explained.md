Around the building and raiding sits a small set of rules that decide who you fight, what a win is worth and what happens after someone hits your base. They fit on one page. This is that page.

## Trophies

Trophies are the score. You win them by earning stars in raids and lose them when someone earns stars against you.

{{table:trophies}}

Notice the gap: the attacker gains twice what the defender loses. A three-star raid gives the raider 30 trophies and costs the defender 15. Trophies are added to the game with every raid, so everybody who plays drifts upwards, and active players climb fastest.

A few details:

- **Attacking is never a risk.** A raid with no stars gives +0. You cannot lose trophies by attacking, only by being attacked.
- **Your count never goes below zero.**
- **Echoes and Outposts pay full trophies** to the attacker, though nobody loses any.
- **Skipping costs none.** Leave or Next before your first unit lands, and it is as if the raid never existed.

## The Ladder

The **Ladder** button on your base opens the top 50 commanders by trophies, with their Citadel levels. If you are among them, your line is highlighted. It is one worldwide list: there are no leagues or seasons today.

## Who you get matched against

When you tap **Attack**, the game looks for a real base that:

- has a Citadel **within one level of yours**, up or down, and
- **you have not raided in the last 12 hours**.

Trophies play no part in it: only the Citadel level does. That keeps fights fair in the sense that matters, since armies and defenses are tied to the Citadel. It also means that rushing your Citadel puts weak guns in front of strong armies.

If there is no such base right now, you get one of two stand-ins:

- an **Echo**: a copy of a real base. The fight is real, the loot is supplied by the game, and the owner loses nothing;
- an **Outpost**: a base built by the computer, at your Citadel level or one below.

The badge on the scouting screen always says which kind you are looking at: **Player**, **Echo** or **Outpost**. The game is new and the player pool is small, so you will see Echoes and Outposts often for now. Nothing about them is hidden.

## Shields

A shield is a break after a bad raid.

- **You get one** when a raid takes {{n:shieldShare}}% or more of the oil and scrap that was on offer.
- **It lasts {{n:shieldHours}} hours.** A timer with a shield appears at the top of your base.
- **While it is up, raids cost you nothing**: no oil, no scrap, no trophies. Your base can still come up as someone's opponent, but for them it behaves like an Echo.
- **Attacking does not break it.** Raid as much as you like under a shield.

A small raid that takes less than {{n:shieldShare}}% gives no shield. That is on purpose: a base that loses little can afford another visit.

There is nothing to buy here. Shields cannot be bought or extended.

## Being raided: what you actually lose

- **Oil and scrap**, within the limits in the [loot guide](/blog/loot-guide-how-much-oil-and-scrap-can-you-steal/).
- **Trophies**, half of what the raider won.
- **Nothing else.** Buildings, walls and Mines are all back at once. There are no repairs and no waiting.

Each raider can hit you for real only once in 12 hours.

## The Log

**Log** on your base lists the last raids on you and by you. A dot on the button means someone has visited since you last looked.

For every defense you see who it was, their Citadel level, the stars, the percentage, what you lost, and two buttons:

- **Replay**: watch the raid as it happened, with all your hidden defenses visible.
- **Revenge**, if it is still available.

A line that says "nothing lost" was a raid on a copy of your base or during a shield.

## Revenge

Someone raided you. For the next {{n:revengeHours}} hours you may hit back.

- **One revenge per raid.** Use it or lose it when the {{n:revengeHours}} hours are up.
- **You attack their base as it is now**, with their current defenses, whatever their Citadel level. Matchmaking rules do not apply.
- **The oil is guaranteed.** The raid holds at least as much oil as they took from you, even if their own tanks are empty.
- **They lose nothing.** Revenge is fought against a copy of their base. The loot comes from the game. So there are no endless feuds: nobody can take revenge for a revenge.
- **You win trophies** like in any raid.

Revenge is the best raid in the game: a known amount of oil, no risk, and a base you have already watched attack you. Before you use it, watch the replay of their raid. You learn how they play, and then you scout their base with the usual thirty seconds.

## Daily rhythm

Put together, the rules suggest a routine:

1. Open the game, **collect**, check the **Log**.
2. If you were raided, watch the replay and take **revenge**.
3. Raid until your stores are full or your builders are all busy. The first win of the day pays {{n:dailyCrystals}} crystals.
4. **Spend**, start the longest upgrades, close the game.

## Common questions

**Can I be raided while I am online?** Yes. A raid is fought against the base as it stood when the raider found it, so nothing you do during those minutes changes the outcome.

**Can I pick a friend to attack?** No. Opponents are chosen by the game, and revenge is the only way to choose a target.

**Can I see who is going to attack me?** No. You find out in the Log.

**Do results ever get rejected?** Every raid is replayed on the server before it counts. That is what stops cheating, and it is explained in [how Dune Clash stops cheating](/blog/fair-play-server-replays-anti-cheat/).
