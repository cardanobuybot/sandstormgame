Dune Clash is a free strategy game for phones and browsers. You build a base on a desert planet, defend it, and raid other players' bases for their oil and scrap. A raid lasts {{n:battleMinutes}} minutes. In about half of them a giant sand worm turns up and eats whatever is walking on open ground.

That is the whole game in four sentences. The rest of this page fills in the details: what you build, how a raid goes, what the worm does, what it costs, and where to get it.

## The short version

- **Kind of game:** build-and-raid strategy. You grow a base over days and weeks and fight short battles against other players' bases.
- **Where:** iPhone (App Store) and any web browser. Android is in testing on Google Play.
- **Price:** free, no ads. You can buy crystals to speed things up, and you also earn them by playing.
- **Size of the game:** {{n:buildingCount}} buildings, {{n:unitCount}} units, {{n:citadelMax}} Citadel levels, two resources.
- **Who makes it:** Sandstorm Game, an independent one-person studio.
- **For whom:** players of 13 and up who like planning a base and then testing it against real people.

## What you do

### Build

You start with a Citadel, one Pumpjack, one Oil Tank, a Barracks and a single Turret, plus {{n:startOil}} oil and {{n:startScrap}} scrap. From there you add buildings, upgrade them, and raise the Citadel itself. Each Citadel level unlocks new buildings and units and lets everything else grow one level higher.

Upgrades take real time, from ten seconds at the start to a few days near the top. Builders limit how many jobs run at once: you have two at Citadel 1 and four by Citadel 7. The [beginner's guide](/blog/dune-clash-beginners-guide/) walks through the first hour.

### Raid

Tap Attack and the game finds another commander near your level. You get {{n:scoutSeconds}} seconds to look the base over, then {{n:battleMinutes}} minutes to take it apart. You choose which units land and where; after that they act on their own.

A raid is scored in stars: one for destroying half the base, one for the Citadel, one for destroying everything. Stars bring trophies. Buildings that hold oil and scrap give them up as they take damage, and that is how your own base gets paid for.

Your army costs nothing. There is no training queue and no waiting: every raid you may land any mix of units that fits your army space. More on that in the [units guide](/blog/dune-clash-units-guide/).

### Defend

While you are away, other players raid you the same way. They hit a snapshot of your base, so nothing needs rebuilding afterwards, but they carry off a share of your oil and scrap and some of your trophies. Walls, five kinds of guns, mines and two hidden launchers decide how much they get. The battle log keeps a replay of every attack, so you can watch exactly where the base gave way.

## Two resources, two jobs

**Oil** runs the base: the Citadel, pumps and tanks, army buildings and unit research are all paid with it. **Scrap** is cut out of spaceships that crashed on the planet, and it pays for everything that defends: guns, walls, mines and snares.

Because they have separate stores, a base can be swimming in oil and still be short of scrap. The [economy guide](/blog/oil-and-scrap-economy-guide/) explains how to keep both flowing.

## The Sandmaw

The thing that sets Dune Clash apart is the worm. The Sandmaw is neutral: it belongs to nobody. It travels under the sand toward footsteps and engine noise, bursts out beneath ground troops and bites everything within reach. It ignores buildings and cannot touch aircraft.

While you scout, a tremor reading tells you whether the worm is coming in this battle. If it does, you have a decision to make: bring more aircraft, land late, or bring enough firepower to kill it when it surfaces. The [Sandmaw guide](/blog/how-to-beat-the-sandmaw/) has the numbers.

## What is different about it

- **A monster in a player-versus-player game.** Most base builders are you against a base. Here there is a third party that does not care who wins.
- **No training timers.** Units are free and ready. You can raid again as soon as the last raid ends.
- **Infantry, vehicles and aircraft together.** Each defense is good against some and blind to others, so army choice matters more than army size.
- **Results cannot be faked.** The server replays every raid from the orders you gave. [Here is how that works](/blog/fair-play-server-replays-anti-cheat/).
- **No ads.** None, anywhere.

## What it does not have yet

Dune Clash is a new game from a very small studio, and some things you may expect are not there: no clans, no chat, no languages other than English. Clans are the next big feature planned. While there are still few players, some of the bases you raid are labelled copies of real bases (Echoes) or computer-built Outposts; the game always tells you which.

## What it costs

Nothing to download and nothing to play. Crystals, the premium currency, finish a build at once or top up missing oil and scrap. You get {{n:startCrystals}} to start, more for each Citadel level and {{n:dailyCrystals}} for your first won raid every day. Packs of crystals are sold in the game from $0.99. The [crystals guide](/blog/dune-clash-crystals-guide/) lists every price.

## Where to play

- **iPhone:** on the App Store, for iOS 15 or later. The game has not reached the stores of EU countries yet.
- **Any browser:** the full game runs on the web with nothing to install, on a phone or a computer.
- **Android:** in closed testing on Google Play, opening to everyone soon.

Details, links and what to do if the store says "not available" are in [how to download Dune Clash](/blog/how-to-download-dune-clash/).
